Questions to ask a dealer before you buy an American-made walk-in tub

Published by Aging Safely Baths. Facts checked October 15, 2026; questions added October 22, 2026.

Direct answer: Ask for the manufacturer's name, the exact model, and the city where that model is built. Then ask whether the origin claim covers the whole tub or only assembly, where the door seal, pumps and heater come from, who holds the warranty, and whether parts are stocked in the U.S. Get the answers on the quote, in writing.

A good dealer will answer every question here without fuss, and many answers are already in the maker's spec sheet. Print this page or keep it open on your phone during the in-home visit.

Where it is made

  • "Who manufactures this tub, and what is the exact model number?" A dealer brand name on the brochure is not always the factory.
  • "Where is this model built? Which city and state?" Makers that build here usually name the plant.
  • "Is that a Made in USA claim or an assembled in USA claim?" The difference between those two labels is the whole point.
  • "Where are the shell and the door formed?" These are the largest and most important parts.
  • "Where do the pump, blower, heater and faucet come from?" On a jetted tub, these are significant to function.
  • "Can you show me that in writing from the manufacturer?" A spec sheet, catalog page or letter is better than a verbal promise.

Warranty and parts

  • "Who holds the warranty, the manufacturer or you?" Most tub warranties come from the maker; installation workmanship is usually separate.
  • "What is covered, for how long, part by part?" Shell, door seal, electrical parts and faucets often have different terms.
  • "Does the warranty require a licensed plumber or electrician?" Some do, and say so in writing.
  • "Is it transferable if I sell the house?" Some are for the first purchaser only.
  • "Who pays labor for a warranty repair?" Published warranties often exclude removal and reinstallation costs.
  • "If the door seal or pump fails in year eight, where does the part ship from and how long does it take?" Read more in our guide to walk-in tub warranties and parts.

The tub itself

  • "How high is the step-in threshold on this model?" It is usually a few inches, far lower than a standard tub wall, but it varies. Compare it with how high you can comfortably lift your feet. If any step is a problem, a roll-in shower may suit you better.
  • "Does the door swing in or out?" Inward doors need no floor space outside the tub and suit small rooms. Outward doors make getting in and out easier for walker users or with a caregiver, but need clear floor space in front.
  • "How long does it take to fill and to drain?" You sit in the tub while it fills and while it drains.
  • "Will it fit through my doors and in my current tub space?" Ask for the outside dimensions and the delivery path.
  • "Does my water heater have enough capacity?" Deep tubs hold more water than a standard bath. Ask for the model's gallon capacity.

The installer and the paperwork

  • "Who does the installation, your staff or a subcontractor, and are they licensed where I live?"
  • "Does the job need permits, and who pulls them?"
  • "Is the electrical work included?" Jetted and heated tubs often need a dedicated circuit.
  • "Is the price itemized?" An itemized quote (tub, options, labor, plumbing, electrical, permits) is easier to compare, and veterans applying for a VA HISA grant need one.
  • "What will I receive at the end?" Ask for the warranty registration, the manual, the model and serial number, and the installer's contact details.

Red flags

  • A flag or "American company" offered as the answer to "where is it made?"
  • A price that is only good if you sign today.
  • No model number on the quote, only a series or a house brand.
  • Warranty terms described verbally but not provided in writing.
  • Reluctance to name the manufacturer, so you cannot check its site yourself.

Once you have answers, compare them against what each maker publishes on our walk-in tub manufacturer directory.

Common questions

What is the first thing to ask a walk-in tub dealer?

Ask for the manufacturer's name, the exact model number and the city where that model is built. A dealer brand name on a brochure is not always the factory.

What are red flags when buying a walk-in tub?

A flag or "American company" offered as the answer to where it is made, a price that only lasts if you sign today, no model number on the quote, warranty terms given only verbally, and reluctance to name the manufacturer.

Should the dealer's answers be in writing?

Yes. Ask for the answers on the quote, along with an itemized price. Veterans applying for a VA HISA grant need an itemized estimate anyway.
