American-made roll-in showers and barrier-free shower pans

By the Aging Safely Baths team. Published October 22, 2026. Facts and links checked October 22, 2026.

Direct answer: Yes, you can buy a roll-in shower built in the United States. Bestbath says its products are "proudly made in Caldwell, Idaho and Sparta, Tennessee," and Showers4Less sells 52 of its pans and packages, from 36 x 36 to 72 x 48 inches. Pick the size first, then confirm where the valve, seat and grab bars come from.

Most of this site is about walk-in tubs. But many families who start out shopping for a tub end up choosing a roll-in shower instead, especially when someone uses a wheelchair, a walker or a shower chair. This page covers the same question for showers: who actually builds them here, what the label covers, and which American-made size fits your bathroom.

We only describe makers whose manufacturing statements we have read on their own websites. For roll-in and barrier-free showers, the maker we sell and can document is Bestbath. On its About page, checked October 22, 2026, the company states:

What does "Made in USA" cover on a shower kit?

A roll-in shower is a system, not a single part. A complete package usually includes a molded pan (the floor), wall panels, and sometimes a seat, grab bars, a valve, a hand-held shower, a curtain rod or glass, and a collapsible water dam. The pan and walls are what a shower maker molds in its own plant. Valves, hand showers, seats and grab bars are often bought in from other suppliers by whoever puts the package together.

That matters because of how the Federal Trade Commission defines the claim. An unqualified "Made in USA" claim means the final assembly or processing happens in the U.S., all significant processing happens in the U.S., and "all or virtually all ingredients or components of the product are made and sourced in the United States" (16 CFR 323.2). The same rule covers online listings and catalogs, not only the sticker on the box (16 CFR 323.3).

So the useful question is not "is it American?" but "which parts of this package are American?" A fair answer from a seller sounds like: the pan and walls are molded at the maker's plant, and here is where the valve and seat come from. Our guide to what "Made in USA" legally means explains the wording in more depth, and our new guide to reading country-of-origin marks covers the imported side of the label.

What size roll-in shower do you need?

Size decides more about how a shower works than any other choice. If the shower is for a public building or a project that must meet the 2010 ADA Standards, those standards set minimum clear inside dimensions. In a private home they are not required, but they are a sensible yardstick for wheelchair use.

Transfer type (608.2.1)36 x 36 inches36 inches wide minimum½ inch maximum, beveled, rounded or vertical

Source: U.S. Access Board, 2010 ADA Standards, section 608. The standards also call for a 30 x 60 inch clear floor space beside a standard roll-in shower, a hand-held spray with a hose at least 59 inches long, and water no hotter than 120°F.

One caution. ADA sizes are measured inside the finished shower, at the center points of opposing sides. A pan sold as "60 x 30" is a nominal footprint, and once walls are on, the inside can be a little smaller. If a project has to meet the ADA Standards, check the inside dimensions on the product's spec sheet.

Pan only, walls, or the full package?

American-made showers are sold three ways, and the right one depends on who is doing the work.

  • Pan only. Just the floor. Use it when an installer will tile the walls or reuse wall panels you already have. Showers4Less lists these under barrier-free shower pans.
  • Full package. Pan, walls and the pieces that go with them, so the parts are designed to fit together. Most of the roll-in sizes above come this way.
  • Designer packages. The same idea with decorative wall finishes, for a bathroom that should not look clinical.

Whichever you choose, ask what is in the box before you compare prices. Two packages with the same footprint can differ by a seat, a valve, a hand shower, grab bars and a curtain or glass.

What should you ask before you order?

  • Which plant molded the pan and the walls, and is that in writing on the listing or spec sheet?
  • Which items in this package are bought in, such as the valve, hand shower, seat, grab bars and glass, and where are they made?
  • What are the clear inside dimensions once the walls are installed?
  • Where is the drain (center, end or trench), and does it line up with my existing drain?
  • What is the threshold height at the entry, and is a collapsible water dam included?
  • What does the warranty cover, and does it depend on who installs it? Our warranty and parts guide lists the clauses to look for.

Common questions

Are there roll-in showers made in the USA?

Yes. Bestbath states that its products are made in Caldwell, Idaho and Sparta, Tennessee, and it makes barrier-free pans and roll-in shower packages. Showers4Less listed 52 Bestbath products in its Made in USA collection on October 22, 2026, in footprints from 36 x 36 to 72 x 48 inches.

Does "Made in USA" on a shower package mean every part is American?

Under the FTC rule, an unqualified "Made in USA" claim means all or virtually all of the product is made and sourced in the U.S. In practice, ask which items in a package are bought in, such as valves, seats and grab bars, because packages are put together from several suppliers.

What size is a standard roll-in shower?

Under the 2010 ADA Standards, a standard roll-in shower is at least 30 inches wide and 60 inches deep inside, with a 60-inch-wide entry and a threshold no higher than half an inch. An alternate roll-in type is at least 36 by 60 inches. A transfer shower is 36 by 36 inches.

Can an American-made roll-in shower replace my bathtub?

Usually, yes. Pans from 60 x 29 to 60 x 36 inches are sized for the five-foot alcove a bathtub normally fills. Check the drain location and the floor before you order, because the drain may need to move.

Does the VA HISA grant require an American-made shower?

No. We found no domestic-content requirement in the HISA application rule at 38 CFR 17.3120. The VA does list roll-in showers as an example of an eligible bathroom change. Buying American is your choice to make.
